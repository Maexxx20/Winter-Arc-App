NORDWAND

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 October 2026

Nordwand is an app for tracking your “Winter Arc” – a personal challenge with your own rules. This policy explains which data the app processes, why, and where it is stored. It is based on the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection (FADP) and, where applicable, the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). The German version is authoritative; this is a translation.

2. Without an account: everything stays on your device

You can use Nordwand fully without an account. In that case your arcs, rules, check-offs, notes, weekly reviews, photos and settings are stored only locally on your device. We receive none of it.

Calculated on your device (with or without an account): the arc recap, the before/after comparison from your journal photos and the statistics are created on your device only. They are passed on only if you share an image yourself via the share sheet – you decide where it goes.

Data export: Under Profile → Export data you can create a file (CSV or JSON) with your arcs, check-offs, notes and reviews. The file is created on your device and handed to the destination you choose via the share sheet (e.g. Files or Mail). Nothing is sent to us. Photos are not included in the export.

Quick actions (long-pressing the app icon) only open sections of the app; no data is transmitted.

3. With an account: backup and sync

If you create an account, we process:

  • Email address – for signing in with a one-time code. There is no password.
  • Your arc data – arcs, rules, contract (including the name you sign with), daily check-offs and amounts, notes, journal photos and weekly reviews. Purpose: backup and use on several devices.
  • Profile – display name, motto (a short sentence about you), Instagram handle and profile picture, if you provide them. All of this is optional. In addition, the list of your badges (e.g. “7 days in a row” with date), which the app calculates from your data.
  • Device token and language for notifications – only if you turn on crew notifications (see section 6).
  • Blocked people – whom you have blocked, with that person’s name at the time of blocking (see section 4). Only you can see this list.

Photos are downscaled before upload (journal photos to at most 1600 pixels, profile pictures to 512 × 512 pixels). Only you can see your journal photos. Reminders – including per-rule reminders – are scheduled locally on the device; no data is transmitted for them. The time of a rule reminder is part of the rule and, with an account, is backed up together with it.

4. Crews

If you join or create a crew, the other members of that crew can see:

  • your profile: display name, profile picture, motto, Instagram handle (if provided) and your badges,
  • a daily summary: whether you held the day, how many of your rules are done, day in the arc, current and best streak, and your completion rate,
  • reactions (emojis) you send them and weekly challenges you start,
  • Crew arcs: if you publish a crew arc as a template, the crew sees its title, period, the template’s rules, your “why” for it and that the template comes from you. If you adopt a template and sign it, the crew sees that and when you signed – and, in your daily summary, which of the template’s rules you completed that day.

Nudges: if your day is still open, a crew member can nudge you – at most once per day. You then receive a notification with their name (if you have turned on crew notifications). To make sure only one such notification is sent per day, the database records who triggered a notification for whom on which day (nudge, reaction, joining, crew arc). These entries are deleted automatically after about a week and are not visible to anyone in the app – except that you can see whom you have already nudged today.

Your crew cannot see your own rules (except the rules of an adopted crew arc template, see above), notes, photos, weekly or arc recaps, or your email address. If you leave a crew, it no longer sees your summaries.

Blocking: you can block a person. Blocking works both ways: neither of you can see the other’s profile or daily status, you cannot send each other reactions or nudges, and you receive no notifications from each other. In a crew you share, it remains visible that you are both members. So that you can find the person in your list again, we store the name they had when you blocked them; only you can see this list (Profile → Blocked people).

Removing members: whoever created a crew can remove members and renew the invite code. Removed people cannot rejoin with the code. For this we store their crew name and the date in a list that only the crew’s owner can see – until the owner lets the person back in or the crew is deleted.

5. Apple Health, Health Connect and Strava (optional)

You can have individual rules checked off automatically, e.g. “10,000 steps” or “Workout”. To do so, connect Nordwand under Profile → Connections with Apple Health (iPhone), Health Connect (Android) and/or Strava. Without a connection, Nordwand reads no health data.

  • What is read: steps, workouts (start and duration), sleep, water intake and mindful minutes – only the types you allow, and only for today and the two days before. Nordwand writes nothing to Apple Health or Health Connect.
  • Where it is calculated: values from Apple Health and Health Connect are read and compared on your device only. They are not transmitted to us. Only the result for the linked rule ends up in your arc data – a check-off or, for amount rules, the value (e.g. “8,432 steps”). With an account, this result is backed up like any other check-off (section 3); your crew only sees the summary (section 4).
  • Strava: if you connect Strava, you sign in with Strava (Strava, Inc., USA) and allow Nordwand to read your activities. This requires an account. On our server we store the Strava access keys, your Strava athlete ID and your name on Strava, as well as a list of your activities from the last 14 days (start, duration, sport type), which is replaced with every request. The app itself cannot read the access keys. We use Strava data only to check off your workout rules.
  • No sharing: health and workout data are not used for advertising, not passed on to third parties and not stored in iCloud.
  • Disconnecting: under Profile → Connections you can end any connection. For Strava, we then delete the access keys and the activity list and revoke the authorisation with Strava. You revoke access to Apple Health or Health Connect in your device settings.

Widgets: the widgets on the home and lock screen (iPhone) and on the home screen (Android) show the arc day, streak and your daily rules. The data for them stays on your device and is not transmitted. On the lock screen it is visible even when the device is locked.

6. Where the data is stored

Account and sync data are stored with Supabase (Supabase Inc., USA) on servers in the Zurich (Switzerland) region. Supabase processes the data on our behalf. Access is technically restricted so that each person can read only their own data plus the profile, crew summaries and crew arcs of their crew members; the profile and daily status of people with whom a block exists are locked. Profile pictures and photos are kept in non-public storage and can only be retrieved via time-limited links. Emails with the sign-in code are sent via the email delivery service Brevo (Sendinblue SAS, France); only your email address is transmitted.

Crew notifications (optional): if you turn them on, we store a device token and the language the app uses on that device (German, English, French or Italian) so that the notification arrives in your language. If someone in your crew reacts, joins, starts a crew arc or nudges you, our database sends a notification to this token via the push service of Expo (650 Industries, Inc., USA) and Apple or Google. Only the token, the crew name and the short notification text are transmitted (e.g. “Lara sent you 🔥” or “Lara is nudging you – something is still open today.”). People with whom a block exists do not trigger notifications for you. If you turn notifications off or sign out, the token is deleted.

App updates (EAS Update): Nordwand downloads smaller app updates (e.g. bug fixes and texts) at launch directly from the update service of Expo (650 Industries, Inc., USA; servers at expo.dev) instead of via the App Store or Google Play. Only technical information is transmitted: platform (iOS or Android), app and runtime version, update channel, the identifier of the installed update and a random installation ID that the update function generates once on the device (for rolling out updates gradually). As with any request on the internet, the server also sees your IP address. No account, arc or health data is transmitted, and the installation ID is not linked to your account.

7. No tracking, no advertising

Nordwand contains no advertising, no analytics or tracking tools, and does not pass any data to third parties for advertising or marketing purposes.

8. Permissions

  • Notifications – only for the reminders (general and per rule) and crew notifications you turn on.
  • Camera and photos – only when you add a photo to a day or choose a profile picture.
  • Apple Health / Health Connect – read-only and only if you turn on the connection (see section 5).

9. Retention and deletion

Data is stored for as long as your account exists. You can delete your account at any time in the app under Profile → Account & sync → Delete account. This immediately and permanently deletes your account, your profile picture, your photos, any Strava connection, your signatures on crew arcs, your list of blocked people and all related data on the server. Crews, weekly challenges and crew arc templates you created remain available to the other members but are no longer linked to your account. You delete data on your device under Profile → Delete all data or by removing the app.

10. Your rights

You have the right to access your data and to have it corrected, deleted and handed over. To do so, write to the email address above. You can also export your arc data yourself at any time (see section 2). You can also lodge a complaint with the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC) or with the competent supervisory authority in your country.
